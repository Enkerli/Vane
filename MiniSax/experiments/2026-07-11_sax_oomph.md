# Experiment 2026-07-11 — v0.2: sax "oomph" vs the Silverwood snippet

## Goal

Close the gap the user heard against the Silverwood tenor snippet
(`references/silverwood/SilvSnip.wav`): the v0.1 voice was clarinet-like.
Descriptor analysis of the snippet pinned the gap down: **H2 nearly as
strong as H1** (−0.2 dB) with a flat harmonic plateau (−6..−8 dB out to
H8) and centroid ≈ 1.5 kHz — a conical-bore spectrum.  v0.1's quarter-wave
bore produces almost no even harmonics at all.

Also integrated: 6 user presets (subtone_003 … singing_vibrato_008) and
2 user articulation suites (attacks/releases 002, legato/gestures 003).

## Starting point

v0.1: half-period clarinet loop, correct register everywhere, odd-only
spectrum.

## Changes tried

1. **STK Saxofony-style two-segment bore** (full-period loop split at a
   blow position; even harmonics from the readout comb like plucking
   position on a string).  With an output tap blend, radiation lowpass and
   a pitch-tracked fundamental boost, a grid search matched the reference
   profile to within ~2 dB on H2..H5.  **Rejected**: register stability.
   The Goertzel-verified grid (notes 196–440 Hz × damping 0.2/0.34 ×
   breath 0.4/0.62/0.85) showed flips to the 2nd register over most of the
   space — the loop's playable pressure window (speaks ≈ 0.74, flips by
   ≈ 1.05) is inherently narrow, and a memoryless reed table has no way to
   hold the low register the way a player's vocal tract does.  A reed-
   dynamics lowpass on the pressure difference made it worse (phase lag).
2. **Static waveshaper (x + a·x²) after the stable v0.1 loop.**  Nearly
   useless in practice: the loop signal is close to a 50%-duty square, and
   any static shaper of a square is another square — x² is almost pure DC.
   Measured: H2 stuck at −23 dB even at a = 2.4.
3. **Self ring-mod against a quarter-period bore tap** (final):
   `y = x + a·x·boreTap(T/8)`.  For a square-ish wave the product is a
   25%-duty rectangle at 2·f0 (strong H2/H4/H6); for a sine it is pure H2.
   The loop is untouched, so register stability is exactly v0.1's.
   Grid-searched `a`, radiation cutoff, brightness against the reference:
   shape 2.0, radiation LP 4 kHz, no fundamental boost needed.

## Results

- `tenor_sax_009` (conicalAmount 0.8, brightness 0.9, damping 0.2), C4
  long tone vs reference: **H2 −0.1 dB vs −0.2 dB**, H3 −10.1 vs −7.8,
  H4 −4.2 vs −6.4, centroid 1851 vs 1471 Hz, even/odd +10.1 vs +5.4 dB.
  H5+ runs several dB shy — the loop's 1/k rolloff vs the reference's
  plateau; preset-space brightness/noise narrows it.
- Full matrix (10 preset × suite runs, 72 tests): every test sounds at the
  correct fundamental (no register flips), no silence, no clipping, no
  non-finite samples.  User presets get H2 ≈ −3..−8 dB at the default
  conicalAmount 0.55.
- Analyzer grew `harmonicProfileDb`, `h2RelH1Db`, `evenOddRatioDb`
  descriptors, and the pitch tracker now picks the smallest strong
  autocorrelation lag (bare argmax octave-errs when H2 rivals H1).
- New engine constant tuning hook: build with `-DMINISAX_TUNING` to make
  the voicing constants mutable for probe sweeps.

## Interesting presets saved

- `tenor_sax_009` — reference-tuned sax body (the "oomph" anchor).
- User presets 003–008 integrated unchanged; all benefit from the default
  conical blend.

## Problems

- H5..H8 several dB below the reference plateau (1/k law of the loop).
- The tracked pitch runs ~+10–25 cents sharp (unchanged from v0.1; pitch
  correction table still pending).
- Tonguing is still approximated by breath gestures (no dedicated events).
- The Saxofony bore is genuinely interesting (real overblow/squeak
  behaviour) — worth revisiting later as an opt-in `boreModel` variant
  with a register-hole mechanism, rather than as the default.

## Next steps

- Formant/plateau shaping (second-order body resonances) to lift H5..H8.
- Pitch correction table (delay compensation vs frequency and damping).
- Dedicated articulation events (tongue, slap, flutter) as DSP features.
- Phase 5 comparison reports (two render dirs; render vs reference
  profile) as a first-class tool.
